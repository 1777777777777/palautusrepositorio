A retrospective is meant to get team members talking. The idea is to discuss how the sprint went and what future improvements to make. There are a lot of ways to approach this, but the course presents three techniques. There's the "Start, Stop, Continue, More of, Less of Wheel". The name is pretty self-explanatory. There's also "Glad, Sad, Mad". There's also a rather round-based model where everyone silently thinks of one good thing, one improvement, and one thing to stop before agreeing on at least three verifiable action items.

Looking at the techniques, there's clearly an effort to avoid finger-pointing and allow for the externalization of issues. "Mad". I do not like this. It feels dishonest. Self-critique, in the best of traditions, should be critical. You should be able to freely criticize and receive criticism. It might sting. But if a team has to invent elaborate schemes just to avoid hurt feelings, it points to more fundamental issues in the group dynamics.

People should feel like they can bring issues up, but that should not mean eliminating any and all tension. Honest reflection doesn't have to feel comfy. If there is no tension, there are no stakes. And if there are no stakes, it starts to feel like no one cares about anything. Please care. A good retrospective shouldn't feel suffocating, stuffy, sanitized.
